Decide: good or defect

With the brick held still in front of the front camera at WP2, an anomaly model scores the photo. A brick with a pen cross scores high; a clean brick scores low. The smallest cross the model still catches is the number that matters most.

How it will work

  1. One PatchCore model per brick, trained only on the depth-masked WP2 good images of that brick. The brick's colour selects the model.
  2. Threshold from the defect images. The 10–20 defect photos per brick set where good ends and defect begins; they are for evaluation and calibration only, never for training.
  3. Measure the smallest cross detected, drawing crosses from the smallest up.
  4. Export to OpenVINO and show the live score and heat-map on this page.

Why this way

Holding the brick gives the same pose every time, close to the camera: well-aligned views for PatchCore and more pixels per millimetre for small crosses. The gripper fingers look the same in every image, so they count as normal. The depth mask greys out people moving behind the brick.

Accuracy first, speed second: every change has to earn its place on a held-out test set.
